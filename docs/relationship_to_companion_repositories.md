# Relationship to companion repositories

## Purpose

Two TP53 repositories are retained because they answer different questions.
They must not be presented as independent replications of the same analysis.

| Repository | Primary question | Dataset focus | Main evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Elephant-TP53-Hotspot-Mapping` | How do human TP53 hotspots map onto elephant TP53 and TP53-related sequences? | Human–elephant focused | Pairwise mapping, exact residue identity, EleProtect prototype, associated published chapter |
| `TP53-Evolutionary-Conservation-Mammals` | Are recurrent human TP53 hotspots unusually constrained across mammals? | Expanded 56-sequence mammalian dataset | Multiple-sequence alignment, residue conservation, mutation recurrence, permutation tests and phylogeny |

## Correct cross-referencing

The elephant repository may cite the mammalian repository as a broader
extension. The mammalian repository may cite the elephant project as the
focused published foundation. Neither repository should copy the other's
result tables or imply that one dataset directly validates the other.

## EleProtect boundary

EleProtect is retained here because it communicates the elephant-focused
sequence analysis. It is an exploratory research prototype, not a clinical
decision-support tool and not evidence that its prioritization has been
experimentally validated.

## Portfolio narrative

Together the projects show a progression from a focused human–elephant mapping
question to a broader mammalian evolutionary-constraint study. Their value
comes from this explicit progression, not from inflating the number of similar
repositories.
