# 🧬 23. Relationship Between Hotspot Conservation and Sequence Similarity

An important distinction should be maintained between:

### Global sequence similarity

```text
identity_to_human
```

and:

### Local hotspot conservation

```text
hotspot-specific conservation
```

A sequence can exhibit moderate overall identity while retaining strong
conservation at particular functionally important positions.

Therefore, the hotspot analysis and global sequence-similarity analysis provide
complementary rather than interchangeable measurements.

The observed high conservation of the six selected TP53 hotspot positions
provides a focused residue-level observation, whereas the identity analysis
describes broader sequence similarity.

---

# 🧠 24. Biological Interpretation

The computational results indicate that the selected TP53 mutation hotspot
positions are highly conserved within the analyzed sequence comparison.

This observation is consistent with the possibility that these residues are
subject to evolutionary constraint.

However, sequence conservation alone cannot establish the mechanism underlying
elephant cancer resistance or Peto's paradox.

The appropriate interpretation is therefore:

```text
Observed sequence conservation
             ↓
Evidence of sequence-level constraint
             ↓
Potential evolutionary significance
             ↓
Testable biological hypothesis
```

and **not**:

```text
Sequence conservation
        ↓
Confirmed cancer resistance mechanism
```

The latter requires independent functional and experimental evidence.

---

# ⚠️ 25. Interpretation Boundaries

The current computational results do **not** independently establish:

- ❌ that conserved TP53 residues cause elephant cancer resistance;
- ❌ that sequence conservation explains Peto's paradox;
- ❌ that every analyzed sequence is functionally equivalent to human TP53;
- ❌ that BLOSUM62 values represent pathogenicity;
- ❌ that computational clusters represent evolutionary lineages;
- ❌ that sequence similarity predicts tumour-suppressor activity;
- ❌ that the exploratory ranking identifies biologically superior TP53
  candidates.

The results should instead be regarded as **sequence-level computational
evidence and hypothesis-generating observations**.

# 🧪 31. Statistical and Computational Caveats

The current results are primarily **descriptive and exploratory**.

The dataset should not be treated as a statistically representative sample of
all elephant TP53 sequences or all mammalian TP53 homologues.

In particular:

1. Sequence identity is dependent on the selected sequences and alignment.
2. Sequence length differences may influence similarity interpretation.
3. Feature-based clustering depends on the selected features and clustering
   implementation.
4. Hotspot conservation depends on the alignment and residue-position mapping.
5. BLOSUM62 scores quantify amino-acid substitution similarity rather than
   experimental functional consequences.
6. The exploratory ranking is not a validated predictive model.
7. Biological conclusions require annotation and independent validation.


# 🧠 34. Overall Interpretation

The principal computational observation is that the six selected human TP53
mutation hotspot positions demonstrate **high conservation in the analyzed
sequence comparison**, ranging from **90.91% to 100.00%**.

At the same time, the broader sequence dataset displays substantial variation
in:

- sequence length;
- amino-acid composition;
- sequence identity;
- feature-space representation;
- computational cluster assignment.

This combination suggests that global sequence divergence and local residue
conservation should be evaluated separately.

The results therefore support further investigation of evolutionary constraint
around TP53 hotspot positions, but they do not independently establish a
causal mechanism for cancer resistance.
